# Silicone Candle Moulds: Hardness, Detail and Lifespan

The explosion of sculptural, geometric, and architectural pillar candles across Indian lifestyle boutiques and social media ateliers has transformed candle making from traditional container pouring into a sophisticated casting art. Behind every intricate ribbed pillar, twisted taper, bubble cube, or anatomical bust is a flexible silicone mould.

Unlike rigid aluminium or polycarbonate moulds that require strict geometric draft angles so a wax pillar can slide out vertically, flexible silicone moulds stretch and peel away from deep undercuts, negative draft profiles, and delicate surface textures.

However, not all silicone moulds behave identically in production. Differences in cure chemistry, Shore durometer hardness, elongation tolerance, and maintenance practices dictate whether a mould produces 20 casts before tearing or withstands hundreds of production cycles in a working commercial studio.

This guide provides a comprehensive technical breakdown of silicone mould chemistry, Shore A durometer ratings, wicking protocols, demoulding techniques, and studio maintenance tailored for Indian makers.

## 1. Chemistry Fundamentals: Tin-Cure vs. Platinum-Cure Silicone

Room-temperature-vulcanizing (RTV) silicones used for candle and soap moulds fall into two distinct chemical categories:

### Tin-Cure (Condensation-Cure) Silicone
Tin-catalyzed silicones cure through a condensation reaction that releases alcohol byproducts as they vulcanize:
- **Advantages:** Lower raw material cost; highly resistant to cure inhibition when casting against master models containing sulfur, latex, or certain resins.
- **Limitations:** Higher linear shrinkage rate (typically 0.2% to 0.6% over time); shorter shelf life; subject to reversion (gradual breakdown and embrittlement) over several years.
- **Production Context:** Frequently utilized for budget mould lines or experimental short-run shapes where maximum longevity is not the primary commercial requirement.

### Platinum-Cure (Addition-Cure) Silicone
Platinum-catalyzed silicones cure through an addition reaction with zero volatile byproducts:
- **Advantages:** Virtually zero long-term shrinkage (typically under 0.1%); superior tear strength and elongation elasticity; excellent thermal resistance up to 200°C; significantly longer operational lifespan.
- **Limitations:** Slightly higher manufacturing cost; sensitive to cure inhibition during the mould-making stage if exposed to amines, sulfur, or tin compounds.

## 2. Understanding Shore Hardness (Durometer Scale)

Shore hardness measures a cured elastomer's resistance to permanent indentation. Candle moulds are classified under the **Shore A** durometer scale:

- **Shore 10A to 15A (Ultra-Soft & Highly Elastic):** Exceptionally stretchy with extreme elongation (often exceeding 500%). Ideal for intricate sculptural candles with deep undercuts, delicate protruding features, or organic figurines. However, because the walls are pliable, tall or heavy moulds can bulge under hydrostatic wax pressure, requiring external support jackets.
- **Shore 20A to 25A (Medium Flexibility):** The balanced benchmark for general candle moulds. Provides sufficient structural rigidity to maintain crisp straight lines without external cradles, while retaining enough elasticity to demould ribbed or geometric shapes cleanly.
- **Shore 30A to 40A (Firm & Rigid):** Stiff moulds suited for simple geometric blocks, wide soap loaves, or shallow decorative trays. Provides razor-sharp edge definition but requires mechanical slicing (a relief seam) to release complex three-dimensional candles.

## 3. Proper Wicking and Demoulding Techniques for Sculptural Moulds

Threading a candle wick through a flexible, sealed silicone mould without damaging the elastomer requires careful studio technique:

### 1. The Fine Wick Puncture Method
Avoid piercing a silicone mould with thick carpet needles or scissors. A large puncture creates a jagged tear that expands under repeated stretching, eventually leaking molten wax. Use a thin upholstery needle or dedicated piercing awl. Thread your braided cotton wick from our [wick systems and hardware catalog](/wholesale/wick-systems-hardware) cleanly through the center.

### 2. Sealing with Reusable Mould Putty
Around the wick exit puncture at the base of the mould, apply a firm seal of reusable, non-staining mould sealer putty (or blue tack). This creates a watertight barrier that prevents liquid wax from leaking across your pour bench.

### 3. Relief Cut Techniques for Complex 3D Figurines
For deep 3D statues or busts where stretching the mould would decapitate or snap fine details:
- Use a sharp scalpel to make a single vertical "relief cut" down one side of the mould (preferably along a natural seam or backside line).
- Cut in a subtle zig-zag pattern rather than a straight line; the interlocking teeth help the mould align itself perfectly when closed.
- Before pouring, wrap several wide elastic bands firmly around the outer mould to hold the slit completely watertight during casting.

## 4. Wax Selection and Thermal Management for Moulds

Pouring into silicone requires a wax formulated with sufficient hardness and volumetric shrinkage to pull cleanly away from silicone walls:

- **Use Dedicated Pillar Waxes:** Container waxes contain softening additives designed for glass adhesion; pouring container soy or soft paraffin into a silicone mould results in broken details and greasy residue clinging to the walls. Formulations like paraffin 140°F+ (such as our [1 kg Paraffin Wax Slab](/products/21)), hardened soy pillar blends, or beeswax naturally contract as they cool, releasing effortlessly from details.
- **Respect Pour Temperatures:** Never pour wax hotter than the silicone's rated thermal limit (typically 80°C to 95°C for standard candle waxes, well below silicone's maximum tolerance). Pouring too hot can cause certain waxes to stick mechanically to silicone micro-pores.
- **Allow Full Internal Cooling:** Demoulding prematurely when the core of the candle is still lukewarm will deform the candle, warp fine ribs, or snap fragile extremities. Allow at least 4 to 8 hours of ambient cooling depending on candle diameter.

## 5. Studio Care, Cleaning, and Storage Protocol in India

Extend the operational lifespan of your silicone moulds with these maintenance rules:

1. **Clean with Warm Water and Mild Soap Only:** Never use aggressive petrochemical solvents, acetone, or isopropyl alcohol to clean wax residues, as solvents extract plasticizers from the silicone matrix, leading to dryness, micro-cracking, and premature tearing.
2. **Never Use Sharp Tools to Pry Wax:** Scratches from metal scissors or craft knives permanently mar the internal silicone finish; every subsequent candle will faithfully reproduce the gouge mark.
3. **Store in Natural Neutral Shape:** Store moulds upright in a dust-free container away from direct sunlight. Stacking heavy objects on flexible moulds will impart permanent flat spots or distorted warping over time.
4. **Apply Periodic Release Conditioning:** While silicone has natural release properties, light application of silicone-compatible release spray before pouring particularly intricate forms reduces friction and prolongs boundary integrity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many candle casts can I expect from a high-quality silicone mould?
With proper care, gentle demoulding, and dedicated pillar waxes, a commercial platinum-cure silicone mould typically yields **200 to 400+ candle casts** before experiencing structural tear fatigue or loss of surface definition.

### Can I repair a torn silicone mould?
Yes. If a tear occurs along an exterior edge or relief slit, clean the torn area thoroughly with isopropyl alcohol, apply a thin bead of liquid RTV silicone or specialized silicone adhesive (like Sil-Poxy), press the torn edges firmly together, and let it cure undisturbed for 24 hours.

### Why does my soy pillar candle crumble or break when unmoulding from silicone?
Soy wax formulated for glass containers is naturally too soft for freestanding pillars. To cast freestanding sculptural candles in silicone moulds, you must use a specialized Pillar Soy Wax (which contains hydrogenated botanical stearates) or blend in 15% to 20% beeswax or paraffin to impart structural rigidity.
