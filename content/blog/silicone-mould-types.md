## The Role of Silicone in Modern Pillar Casting

The explosion of sculptural, geometric, and architectural pillar candles in recent years has transformed candle making from traditional container pouring into an expressive casting discipline. Behind every intricate ribbed pillar, twisted taper, or anatomical bust is a flexible silicone mould.

Unlike rigid aluminium or polycarbonate moulds that require strict geometric draft angles so a wax pillar can slide out vertically, flexible silicone moulds stretch and peel away from deep undercuts, negative draft profiles, and delicate surface textures.

However, not all silicone moulds behave identically in production. Differences in cure chemistry, Shore durometer hardness, elongation tolerance, and maintenance practices dictate whether a mould produces 20 casts before tearing or withstands hundreds of production cycles in a working atelier.

## Chemistry Fundamentals: Tin-Cure vs. Platinum-Cure Silicone

Room-temperature-vulcanizing (RTV) silicones used for candle and soap moulds fall into two chemical categories:

### Tin-Cure (Condensation-Cure) Silicone

Tin-catalyzed silicones cure through a condensation reaction that releases alcohol byproducts as they vulcanize.
- **Advantages:** Lower raw material cost; highly resistant to cure inhibition when casting against master models containing sulfur, latex, or certain resins.
- **Limitations:** Higher linear shrinkage rate (typically 0.2% to 0.6% over time); shorter shelf life; subject to reversion (gradual breakdown and embrittlement) over several years.
- **Production Context:** Frequently utilized for budget mould lines or experimental short-run shapes where maximum longevity is not the primary commercial requirement.

### Platinum-Cure (Addition-Cure) Silicone

Platinum-catalyzed silicones cure through an addition reaction with zero volatile byproducts.
- **Advantages:** Virtually zero long-term shrinkage (typically under 0.1%); superior tear strength and elongation elasticity; excellent thermal resistance up to 200°C; significantly longer operational lifespan.
- **Limitations:** Slightly higher manufacturing cost; sensitive to cure inhibition during the mould-making stage if exposed to amines, sulfur, or tin compounds.
- **Production Context:** The gold standard for commercial craft manufacturing and high-volume artisan production lines where dimensional repeatability and tear resilience are non-negotiable.

## Understanding Shore Hardness (Durometer Scale)

Shore hardness measures a cured elastomer's resistance to permanent indentation. Candle moulds are classified under the **Shore A** durometer scale:

- **Shore 10A to 15A (Ultra-Soft & Elastic):** Exceptionally stretchy with extreme elongation (often exceeding 500%). Ideal for intricate sculptural candles with deep undercuts, delicate protruding features, or organic figurines. However, because the walls are pliable, tall or heavy moulds can bulge under hydrostatic wax pressure, requiring external support jackets.
- **Shore 20A to 25A (Medium Flexibility):** The balanced benchmark for general candle moulds. Provides sufficient structural rigidity to maintain crisp straight lines without external cradles, while retaining enough elasticity to demould ribbed or geometric shapes cleanly.
- **Shore 30A to 40A (Firm & Rigid):** Stiff moulds suited for simple geometric blocks, wide soap loaves, or shallow decorative trays. Provides razor-sharp edge definition but requires mechanical slicing (a relief seam) to release complex three-dimensional candles.

## Proper Wicking Techniques for Silicone Moulds

Threading a candle wick through a flexible, sealed silicone mould without damaging the elastomer requires careful studio technique:

1. **Avoid Oversized Needle Punctures:** Piercing a mould with a thick carpet needle creates a jagged puncture that expands with repeated stretching, eventually causing molten wax leaks or split tears. Use a thin upholstery needle or dedicated wick puncture tool.
2. **Seal with Mould Putty or Blue Tack:** Around the base exit point where the wick penetrates the mould, apply a firm seal of reusable non-staining mould sealer putty to prevent liquid wax seepage onto your pour table.
3. **Pre-Waxed vs. Raw Spooled Wick:** Using raw braided cotton wick spools allows you to pull a continuous tensioned line through the mould, anchoring it tightly with a wick centering bar at the open pouring base.

## Wax Selection and Thermal Management for Moulds

Pouring into silicone requires a wax formulated with sufficient hardness and volumetric shrinkage to pull cleanly away from silicone walls:

- **Use Dedicated Pillar Waxes:** Container waxes contain softening additives designed for glass adhesion; pouring container soy or soft paraffin into a silicone mould results in broken details and greasy residue clinging to the walls. Formulations like paraffin 140°F+, hardened soy pillar blends, or beeswax naturally contract as they cool, releasing effortlessly from details.
- **Respect Pour Temperatures:** Never pour wax hotter than the silicone's rated thermal limit (typically 80°C to 95°C for standard candle waxes, well below silicone's maximum tolerance). Pouring too hot can cause certain waxes to stick mechanically to silicone micro-pores.
- **Allow Full Internal Cooling:** Demoulding prematurely when the core of the candle is still lukewarm will deform the candle, warp fine ribs, or snap fragile extremities. Allow at least 4 to 8 hours of ambient cooling depending on candle diameter.

## Studio Care, Cleaning, and Storage Protocol

Extend the operational lifespan of your silicone moulds with these maintenance rules:

1. **Clean with Warm Water and Mild Soap Only:** Never use aggressive petrochemical solvents, acetone, or isopropyl alcohol to clean wax residues, as solvents extract plasticizers from the silicone matrix, leading to dryness, micro-cracking, and premature tearing.
2. **Never Use Sharp Tools to Pry Wax:** Scratches from metal scissors or craft knives permanently mar the internal silicone finish; every subsequent candle will faithfully reproduce the gouge mark.
3. **Store in Natural Neutral Shape:** Store moulds upright in a dust-free container away from direct sunlight. Stacking heavy objects on flexible moulds will impart permanent flat spots or distorted warping over time.
4. **Apply Periodic Release Conditioning:** While silicone has natural release properties, light application of silicone-compatible release spray before pouring particularly intricate forms reduces friction and prolongs boundary integrity.
