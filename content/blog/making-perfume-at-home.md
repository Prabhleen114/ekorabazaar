# Making Perfume at Home: Carrier Bases, Ratios and Aging

Perfumery has a profound cultural legacy in India, from the ancient steam-distilled deg-bhapka stills of Kannauj to the luxury attars of Lucknow and Hyderabad. In recent years, a vibrant wave of independent Indian makers and beauty entrepreneurs has brought custom fragrance creation into the home studio.

Creating a professional-grade fragrance—whether a lightweight body mist, an intense Eau de Parfum, or a concentrated non-alcoholic roll-on attar—requires more than simply dropping fragrant oils into a bottle. It demands a working knowledge of olfactory pyramids, volatile evaporation rates, carrier base chemistry, maceration timelines, and regulatory compliance.

This guide walks you through the practical, step-by-step process of formulating, blending, and aging fine fragrances at home or in an artisan studio, tailored specifically for the Indian market and climate.

## 1. Choosing Your Carrier: Alcohol-Based vs. Oil-Based

The first formulation decision in perfumery is selecting your carrier medium. Each has distinct sensory attributes, shelf dynamics, and legal considerations in India.

### Alcohol-Based Perfumery (Ethanol)
Alcohol is the worldwide carrier of choice for commercial fine fragrance:
- **Projection and Sillage:** Ethanol has high volatility; as it evaporates rapidly off warm skin, it lifts aroma molecules into the air, creating strong projection and an aromatic trail (sillage).
- **Crisp Top Notes:** Alcohol preserves delicate citrus, green, and floral top notes with maximum brilliance.
- **The Indian Legal Reality:** Pure perfumer's grade alcohol is 95% to 99% Denatured Ethyl Alcohol (SDA-40B or specially denatured spirit). In India, purchasing pure undenatured ethanol is heavily regulated by state excise departments to prevent misuse. For small-scale artisan makers, legally sourcing denatured perfumery-grade alcohol (treated with Bitrex or tertiary butyl alcohol to render it non-potable) through licensed chemical distributors is the compliant route.
- *Tax Context:* Perfumery alcohol and finished perfumes generally fall under the standard 18% GST bracket in India.

### Oil-Based Perfumery (Attar & Roll-Ons)
Oil-based perfumes (traditional *ittar* or modern perfume oils) use non-volatile botanical lipids as the carrier:
- **Skin Intimacy and Longevity:** Because carrier oils do not evaporate into the atmosphere, oil-based perfumes stay close to the skin. They develop an intimate, long-lasting aroma that can linger for 6 to 12 hours.
- **Alcohol-Free Appeal:** Highly popular across diverse Indian communities seeking halal-compliant, non-drying, and sensitive-skin-friendly personal scents.
- **Best Carrier Oils:** Fractionated Coconut Oil (MCT oil) is the gold standard because it is completely odorless, water-clear, non-greasy, and exhibits virtually infinite oxidative shelf life. Jojoba oil is an excellent secondary carrier, though it has a very faint natural wax aroma. Avoid olive oil or unrefined seed oils, which possess strong natural scents that compete with delicate perfumery notes.

## 2. Fragrance Concentrations: From Eau de Cologne to Extrait

The concentration of pure aromatic concentrate (essential oils and aroma chemicals) relative to your carrier determines your fragrance classification:

| Concentration Type | Aromatic Concentrate (% by weight) | Carrier (% by weight) | Average Longevity on Skin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eau de Cologne (EDC)** | 2% – 5% | 95% – 98% (Alcohol + Water) | 1 to 2 hours |
| **Eau de Toilette (EDT)** | 5% – 12% | 88% – 95% (Alcohol) | 3 to 4 hours |
| **Eau de Parfum (EDP)** | 15% – 20% | 80% – 85% (Alcohol) | 5 to 8 hours |
| **Extrait de Parfum / Attar** | 20% – 35% | 65% – 80% (Oil or Alcohol) | 8 to 12+ hours |

For most independent artisan brands launching luxury fragrances in India, **Eau de Parfum (18% to 20%)** or **Concentrated Perfume Oil (25% to 30%)** offers the high perceived value and longevity demanded by Indian consumers who contend with warm tropical climates.

## 3. The Olfactory Architecture: Building the Perfume Pyramid

A balanced perfume evolves dynamically on the skin over several hours. To prevent your blend from smelling flat or disappearing within twenty minutes, formulate across three distinct evaporation layers:

### Top Notes (20% to 30% of concentrate)
- **Molecular Weight:** Lightest; rapid evaporation (first 15 to 30 minutes).
- **Materials:** Bergamot, sweet orange, lemon, cardamom, pink pepper, mint, lavender.
- **Function:** Creates the initial sensory hook when first sprayed or applied to pulse points.

### Heart / Middle Notes (40% to 50% of concentrate)
- **Molecular Weight:** Medium; forms the true personality of the scent (lasts 2 to 4 hours).
- **Materials:** Rose absolute, jasmine sambac (Mogra), neroli, cinnamon bark, geranium, geranium bourbon.

### Base Notes (25% to 35% of concentrate)
- **Molecular Weight:** Heaviest; slow evaporation (lingers 6 to 12+ hours).
- **Materials:** Sandalwood, vetiver (Khus), agarwood (Oud), cedarwood, patchouli, amber resin, vanilla, clean white musks.
- **Function:** Acts as natural fixatives that anchor lighter molecules into the skin or clothing.

## 5. The Critical Aging Phase: Maceration and Maturation

Novice perfume makers often make a batch, spray it immediately, and feel disappointed by a harsh alcohol smell with muddy, disconnected aroma notes. Perfumery requires time for chemical esterification and harmonious molecular blending.

### Phase 1: Maturation (Aging the Pure Concentrate)
Before adding any carrier alcohol or oil, combine your raw aromatic oils together in a sealed amber glass bottle. Store in a dark cupboard at 18°C to 22°C for **7 to 14 days**. During maturation, delicate aldehydes and esters interact chemically, rounding off harsh edges and harmonizing the accord.

### Phase 2: Maceration (Aging with the Carrier)
After blending the matured aromatic concentrate with your perfumer's alcohol or carrier oil, return the sealed bottle to a dark, cool environment for **at least 3 to 6 weeks**.
- Every few days, swirl the bottle gently to encourage uniform dispersion.
- Over 4 weeks, the alcohol's sharp ethanol bite softens, and the fragrance deepens into a smooth, cohesive olfactory experience.
- *Chilling & Filtration (For Alcohol Perfumes):* Plant waxes from natural absolutes can precipitate out, creating a faint haze. Before final bottling, place the matured alcohol perfume in a refrigerator (4°C) for 24 hours, then filter it through unbleached coffee filter paper into sanitized bottles to achieve crystal clarity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use rubbing alcohol (surgical spirit) to make perfume?
No. Rubbing alcohol or surgical spirit sold in Indian pharmacies is typically 70% isopropyl alcohol with strong medicinal, antiseptic chemical odors and added denaturants that ruin delicate floral and citrus notes. Only use 95% to 99% odorless perfumer's denatured ethyl alcohol.

### How do I prevent my oil-based perfume from smelling rancid over time?
Always use saturated, shelf-stable carrier oils such as Fractionated Coconut Oil (MCT oil) or pure jojoba wax. Add 0.1% to 0.2% mixed Tocopherols (Vitamin E) to the finished oil blend as an antioxidant to prevent lipid degradation. Store bottles away from heat and direct sunlight.

### What is the difference between an attar and a modern perfume oil?
Traditionally, an Indian attar (*ittar*) is an all-natural botanical or earthy extract (such as Mitti attar, Gulab, or Ruh Khus) hydro-distilled directly into a pure sandalwood or liquid paraffin base over several weeks. A modern perfume oil typically combines synthetic fine fragrance aroma molecules, natural essential oils, and lightweight fractionated coconut oil.
